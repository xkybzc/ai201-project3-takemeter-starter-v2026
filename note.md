Bracket Analysis: This year, Conference Finalists will be teams seeded #1, #2, #3 & #4. In the last 20 years, it has been the most common seed combination for Conference Finals.
A little analysis of the seeds of NBA Conference Finalists since 1984, the first year where 8 teams were featured in each conference, for those of you who want some bracket statistics.

This year, the conference finalists will be...

Thunder (#1)

Spurs (#2)

Knicks (#3)

Cavaliers (#4)

Since 2007, it is the fifth time this seed combination (1234) has occurred, making it the most common combination of the last two decades. The other four times it occurred were (bolded is the eventual winner)...

2022 (Heat 1, Celtics 2, Warriors 3, Mavericks 4)

2011 (Bulls 1, Heat 2, Mavericks 3, Thunder 4)

2010 (Lakers 1, Magic 2, Suns 3, Celtics 4)

2007 (Pistons 1, Cavaliers 2, Spurs 3, Jazz 4)

(This should make Knicks fans happy)

Now, for other seed combinations, you might think that 1122 is the most common, but you would be wrong: the most common seed combination is 1123, having occurred 12 times (twice as much as any other combination) since 1984. From most to least common, the bracket combinations that we have seen are...

1123 (85, 88, 92, 93, 96, 97, 03, 04, 08, 09, 16, 19) - 12 times

1122 (86, 01, 05, 14, 15, 17) - 6 times

1234 (07, 10, 11, 22, 26) - 5 times

1133 (91, 98, 00, 02) - 4 times

1224 (06, 12, 18) - 3 times

1126 (84, 95) - 2 times

1335 (90, 20) - 2 times

And those that occurred only once (for now):

1136 (89)

1137 (87)

1235 (13)

1228 (99)

1278 (23)

1346 (25)

1356 (24)

Finally, the only two Conference Finals without a single #1 seed making it:

2255 (94) - Hawks and Supersonics were #1 seeds that year, with the Sonics famously losing to the #8 Nuggets in the first round

2345 (21) - Sixers and Jazz were #1 seeds, and both lost in the Conference semifinals.

One thing to note is that since the play-in tournament has been introduced (2021), we have seen 4 "new seed combinations" occur already (+ 1234 twice). Although for my money, I think it means the various salary cap rules are making the league more fair than ever, reducing the gaps between the various top teams.

With the elmination of the L.A. Dodgers, the Los Angeles Lakers 3-peat from 2000-2002 remains the last time a team in the major American league has accomplished such a feat - and there aren't any reigning champions positioned to even repeat.
Yes this is a few days late but the Los Angeles Dodgers join the likes of the 2025 Kansas City Chiefs and the 2023 Tampa Bay Lightning as the latest failed attempts to achieve the ever elusive threepeat, with the Brewers sweeping them after repeated blown leads in every game.

I won't pretend to know much about non-American sports leagues - i'm sure Real Madrid has done it once or twice since 2002 - but as far as the NBA is concerned, I do at least know a few times that a 3peat was thwarted for one reason or another...

2017-2018 Warriors: The most recent failed threepeat is, of course, the KD Warriors who were lacking the snake in the Western Conference Finals and all but one game of the 2019 Finals, ultimately losing in 6 games to the Toronto Raptors.

The Warriors would eventually win another ring while Durant has, to date, yet to get back to the Conference Finals in either the East or West.

Before anyone points to the 2016 Warriors for another failed 3peat between 2015-17, I don't think Kevin Durant joins the Warriors if they don't blow that 3-1 lead so even if Durant doesn't stay in the East, they probably lose in 2017 as that year's Cavaliers team was straight up better than the year before.

2012-13 Miami Heat: Four Finals, two rings. Take your pick of LeBron not choking in the 2011 Finals or the Spurs not being one of the all-time best teams in 2014 due to forces beyond mortal comprehension (I really don't see a world in which that Heatles team wins in 2014 barring injury. The Spurs that year were historic by every metric one measures success.). Point being, the Heatles repeated but LeBron failed to match the feat Jordan did. Twice.

Regardless of the tiresomeness of the GOAT debate, his performance in the 2011 Finals still remains the biggest blemish of James' career.

2009-2010 Lakers: Did you know the Lakers, despite their history of success in the BAA/NBA have only threepeated twice in their entire existence?

The first time they did so was back in the segregation era of the league (1952-54) so it shouldn't count but then there's the dominance of the Kobe/Shaq threepeat.

The Kobe/Gasol duo did their best in 2011 but Dirk's Mavericks dominated throughout the second round and swept them on their way to the franchise's first & thus far only championship. They'd also have threepeated if they won in 2008 but I digress.

After this failed repeat, well we all know what L.A. tried to do with the botched Chris Paul 3-team trade and the infamous pivot via a Dwight/Nash/Kobe Big 3. The only reason one can't say they're the worst example of a failed superteam? At least they (barely) made the playoffs in 2012/13. You can't say the same for the Westbrook Lakers of 2021/22...even if both failed contenders did eventually give out a lottery pick (It was Dyson Daniels for the Pelicans, Mikal Bridges for the Lakers).

2003-07 Spurs: This is an interesting case because San Antonio had two chances to threepeat. Or I suppose they had one chance to 4-peat like the 1997-2000 Houston Comets or 5-peat (Quinpeat?).

As they won in 2003, 2005 & 2007, winning in 2004 or 2006 would have edged out Shaq and company as the most recent threepeat amongst the major leagues, with one of those rings potentially coming at Shaq's expense in the 2006 Finals.

The Spurs would likely be favored in 2004 and 2006 - thought it's worth mentioning that same Pistons team from 2004 sans-future All-Star Mehmet Okur took them to a tight 7 games in 2005 beat the Kobe/Shaq Lakers in 2004 in 5 games.

Ultimately, we'll never know how either series would play out as the Spurs lost in the 2nd round to the eventual Western finalist Dallas Mavericks in 2006 in a hard-fought 7 game series

In 2004, they were trounced in 6 by the final gasp of the Kobe/Shaq Lakers with the new additions of Gary Payton and pedophile Karl Malone who, I kid you not, thought it was a good idea to flirt with Kobe's wife. Truly a class act, that ringless creep.

Still, 3 rings in 5 years is nothing to sneeze at. That's more than Karl Malone got in his 19 years of being the statistically largest choker in playoff history.

Given that no team in the NBA has even made it back to the Finals in the 20s aside from the Celtics (which was non-consecutive in 2022 and 2024), it's likely we won't see one occur for quite some time.
